VaultCam — Terms of Use

Last updated: 14 November 2026

App: VaultCam — Vault & Passwords Package: aoufi.riadh.vaultcam Android 8.0+ Publisher: Riadh Aoufi
In short. VaultCam is a fully offline vault: everything you put in it is encrypted on your phone and never leaves it. There is no account and no server, which has one consequence you must understand before you use it — if you forget your PIN, or uninstall the app, or clear its data, your vault is gone permanently and nobody can recover it, including us. The app is free and shows banner ads on two screens. Keep backups.

They apply together with our Privacy Policy and with Google Play's own terms.

2. What the App is

VaultCam stores photos, videos, notes and passwords on your device inside an encrypted vault, opened with a PIN or your device biometric. It includes a secure camera, albums, encrypted local backups, a break-in log, a decoy vault, a Guardian PIN, a Time Lock, and launcher disguises that make the App appear as a calculator, clock or notepad.

There is no account, no cloud and no server. We operate nothing that your vault could be stored on or recovered from. The App's only network use is to fetch banner advertisements on its Settings and How-to-Use screens.

3. Who may use the App

  • You must be 13 or older. The App is not directed at children under 13.
  • Use it only on a device you own or are authorised to use.
  • You must be able to lawfully use strong encryption where you live. Some countries restrict it; checking that is your responsibility.

4. No recovery — read this before you rely on the App

We cannot unlock your vault, reset your PIN, or recover your files. There is no master key, no backdoor, no reset code and no support procedure that can do it. This is not a limitation we could lift on request; it is how the encryption works, and it is the reason the vault is private in the first place.

Your data is permanently and irrecoverably lost if:

  • you forget your PIN, or forget a backup passphrase;
  • you uninstall the App, or clear its data or storage in Android settings;
  • you reset the device, or the device is lost, stolen, broken or wiped;
  • the vault files are corrupted by a storage fault or by another app.

Android's own cloud backup is deliberately disabled for the App, so your vault is not included in a Google account backup and will not reappear on a new phone.

Backups are your responsibility

The App can write an encrypted backup file (.vcbak) protected by a passphrase you choose, to a location you choose. Use it. We never receive a copy and cannot recover the passphrase. Keep the file and the passphrase somewhere safe and separate, and test a restore before you delete any original.

VaultCam is not a backup service and is not a substitute for one. If a photo matters to you, it should exist somewhere other than inside one phone.

5. Features that refuse access on purpose

Three features are designed to lock you out, and will:

  • Guardian PIN — entering it hard-locks the vault for the duration you chose, up to 24 hours. During that time no PIN opens it, including the correct one. We cannot shorten or cancel it. Nothing is deleted; you simply have to wait.
  • Time Lock — during the hours you set, the correct PIN is refused.
  • Decoy PIN — opens a separate decoy vault. Items saved in the decoy are in the decoy, not in your real vault.

These are working as intended, not faults, and support cannot override them. Set them with that in mind.

6. The break-in log and the camera

If you enable it, a wrong PIN silently takes a photo with the front camera and records the attempt, optionally with a coarse location. Everything stays on your device.

Because that photographs a real person, you are responsible for using it lawfully. Those images are yours and in your control, and you are the one who must comply with whatever your local law requires about recording people, informing them or keeping such images. Do not use the feature to spy on, stalk or harass anyone. Do not install the App on someone else's device to monitor them — that may be a criminal offence where you live.

7. Disguises

The launcher disguises exist so that a casual onlooker does not see a vault on your home screen. They are a privacy feature. They are not for concealing anything from a lawful investigation, from a court order, or from an employer or guardian entitled to inspect a device, and they do not make anything in the vault lawful.

Nor are they absolute: a determined person with access to your unlocked phone, or with forensic tools, may still find the App. Do not treat a disguise as protection against a serious adversary.

8. What you must not store or do

We cannot see your vault, which means we cannot police it — so this is on you. You must not use the App to store or handle:

  • child sexual abuse material, or any sexual content involving a minor — under any circumstances, in any country;
  • intimate images of anyone shared or kept without their consent;
  • material that is stolen, obtained by unauthorised access, or that you have no right to hold — including someone else's credentials, documents or data;
  • anything whose possession is a criminal offence where you are.

You must also not tamper with, decompile, repackage or redistribute the App, or circumvent its ad display or its locks.

Breaking this section ends your right to use the App. Since we hold nothing and can see nothing, the only enforcement available to anyone is by law enforcement, acting against your device, under the law — not by us.

9. Security, honestly stated

The App encrypts vault files with AES-256-GCM, stores its database with SQLCipher, derives keys from your PIN with PBKDF2 and random salts, keeps settings in Android's encrypted preferences, and blocks screenshots on every vault screen. We believe those are sound choices and we have implemented them carefully.

But we do not warrant that the App cannot be broken. Encryption protects data at rest on a device that is itself intact. It cannot protect you if:

  • your PIN is weak, written down, or watched as you type it;
  • the device is rooted, running a modified Android build, or already infected;
  • another app or person has your unlocked phone;
  • you decrypt an item and then share or export it;
  • a flaw is found in the App, in Android, or in a cryptographic library — none of which can be ruled out.

Disguise screens deliberately do not block screenshots, because a calculator that refused them would give itself away.

Ad requests are sent non-personalised, and in the EEA and the UK a consent form is shown before the first ad. No vault content is ever sent to an ad network.
